# Revelation 8

### The seventh seal

<sup>1</sup>And when He opened the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven for about half an hour.[^1]

<sup>2</sup>And I saw the seven angels who stand before God, and seven trumpets were given to them.[^2] <sup>3</sup>Then another angel came and stood at the altar, having a golden censer. Much incense was given to him, so that he might add it to the prayers of all the saints on the golden altar that is before the throne.[^3] <sup>4</sup>From the hand of the angel, the smoke of the incense went up before God with the prayers of the saints.[^4] <sup>5</sup>Then the angel took the censer, filled it with fire from the altar, and cast it onto the earth; and there were thunders, and voices, and flashes of lightning, and an earthquake.[^5]

### The first four trumpets

<sup>6</sup>The seven angels who had the seven trumpets prepared themselves to blow their trumpets.[^6] <sup>7</sup>And the first blew his trumpet: there came hail and fire mixed with blood, and they were cast upon the earth. And a third of the earth was burned up, and a third of the trees were burned up, and all green grass was burned up.[^7]

<sup>8</sup>And the second angel blew his trumpet: something like a great mountain burning with fire was cast into the sea, and a third of the sea became blood,[^8] <sup>9</sup>and a third of the creatures that were in the sea, those that had life, died, and a third of the ships were destroyed.[^9]

<sup>10</sup>And the third angel blew his trumpet: a great star fell from heaven, burning like a torch, and it fell on a third of the rivers and on the springs of the waters.[^10] <sup>11</sup>The star is called Wormwood. A third of the waters became wormwood, and many people died from the waters, because they were made bitter.[^11]

<sup>12</sup>And the fourth angel blew his trumpet: a third of the sun was struck, and a third of the moon, and a third of the stars, so that a third of them would be darkened, and the day would not shine for a third of it, and likewise the night.[^12] <sup>13</sup>And I saw, and I heard an angel flying in midheaven, saying with a loud voice: Woe, woe, woe to those who dwell on the earth, because of the remaining voices of the trumpet of the three angels who are about to blow![^13]

---

[^1]: **8:1** Cf. Hab 2:20; Zeph 1:7; Zech 2:13 (Heb. 2:17).

[^2]: **8:2** “Stand”: Gr. ἑστήκασιν, perfect. “Trumpets”: Gr. σάλπιγγες (cf. 1:10; 4:1; Num 10:9; Josh 6:4; Joel 2:1).

[^3]: **8:3** “At the altar”: Gr. ἐπὶ τοῦ θυσιαστηρίου, lit. “upon the altar”. “Censer”: Gr. λιβανωτός. “That he might add it to the prayers”: lit. “that he might give it to the prayers”. “Golden altar… before the throne”: cf. Ex 30:1–10; 40:5; Lev 16:12–13.

[^4]: **8:4** “With the prayers”: Gr. ταῖς προσευχαῖς, the same dative as in 8:3, rendered there “to the prayers” (cf. 5:8; Ps 141:2).

[^5]: **8:5** “Took”: Gr. εἴληφεν, perfect (cf. 5:7). Cf. Lev 16:12; Ezek 10:2. “Thunders, and voices, and flashes of lightning”: cf. 4:5; 11:19; 16:18; Ex 19:16.

[^6]: **8:6** “Blow their trumpets”: Gr. σαλπίζω, “to sound the trumpet”.

[^7]: **8:7** “Hail and fire”: cf. Ex 9:23–25. “They were cast”: Gr. ἐβλήθη, singular. “Green”: Gr. χλωρός, the same term rendered “pale” in 6:8. Variant: the Textus Receptus reads “the first angel” and omits “and a third of the earth was burned up”.

[^8]: **8:8** “Something like a great mountain”: Gr. ὡς ὄρος μέγα. Cf. Jer 51:25. “Became blood”: cf. Ex 7:20–21. Variant: the Byzantine text omits “with fire”; the Textus Receptus has it.

[^9]: **8:9** “Creatures”: Gr. κτίσματα (cf. 5:13). “That had life”: Gr. τὰ ἔχοντα ψυχάς, lit. “that had souls” (cf. 6:9; 16:3).

[^10]: **8:10** “Torch”: Gr. λαμπάς (cf. 4:5).

[^11]: **8:11** “The star is called Wormwood”: lit. “the name of the star is called: the Wormwood”. “Wormwood”: Gr. ἄψινθος, a bitter-tasting plant. Cf. Jer 9:15 (Heb. 9:14); 23:15; Amos 5:7; Ex 15:23.

[^12]: **8:12** “Was struck”: Gr. πλήσσω. Cf. Ex 10:21–23; Joel 2:10; Amos 8:9.

[^13]: **8:13** “Angel”: follows the Textus Receptus and some manuscripts (ἀγγέλου; cf. 14:6, another angel “flying in midheaven”); the SBLGNT, codices Sinaiticus and Alexandrinus, and the Byzantine text read “eagle” (ἀετοῦ). “Midheaven”: Gr. μεσουράνημα, the highest point of the sky (cf. 14:6; 19:17). “Woe”: Gr. οὐαί (cf. 9:12; 11:14; 12:12). “Remaining”: Gr. λοιπαί.
