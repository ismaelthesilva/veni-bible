# Revelation 11

### The measuring of the sanctuary

<sup>1</sup>A reed like a rod was given to me, and I was told: Rise and measure the sanctuary of God, and the altar, and those who worship in it.[^1] <sup>2</sup>And the court that is outside the sanctuary, leave it out, and do not measure it, for it was given to the nations; and they will trample the holy city for forty-two months.[^2]

### The two witnesses

<sup>3</sup>And I will grant My two witnesses to prophesy for one thousand two hundred and sixty days, clothed in sackcloth.[^3] <sup>4</sup>These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth.[^4] <sup>5</sup>And if anyone wants to harm them, fire comes out of their mouth and devours their enemies; and if anyone would harm them, in this way he must be killed.[^5] <sup>6</sup>These have authority to shut the sky, so that no rain may fall during the days of their prophecy; and they have authority over the waters to turn them into blood, and to strike the earth with every plague, as often as they wish.[^6]

<sup>7</sup>And when they have completed their testimony, the beast that comes up out of the abyss will make war against them, and conquer them, and kill them.[^7] <sup>8</sup>Their dead body will lie in the street of the great city that is spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, where also their Lord was crucified.[^8] <sup>9</sup>Some from the peoples, and tribes, and tongues, and nations look at their dead body for three and a half days, and do not permit their dead bodies to be placed in a tomb.[^9] <sup>10</sup>Those who dwell on the earth rejoice over them and are glad, and will send gifts to one another, because these two prophets tormented those who dwell on the earth.[^10]

<sup>11</sup>After the three and a half days, a breath of life from God entered them, and they stood on their feet, and great fear fell on those who were watching them.[^11] <sup>12</sup>Then they heard a loud voice from heaven saying to them: Come up here. And they went up to heaven in the cloud, and their enemies watched them.[^12] <sup>13</sup>In that hour there was a great earthquake, and a tenth of the city fell, and seven thousand names of men were killed in the earthquake; and the rest were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven.[^13] <sup>14</sup>The second woe has passed; behold, the third woe is coming soon.[^14]

### The seventh trumpet

<sup>15</sup>And the seventh angel blew his trumpet: there were loud voices in heaven, saying:

&emsp;The kingdom of the world has become<br>
&emsp;our Lord’s and His Christ’s,<br>
&emsp;and He will reign forever and ever.[^15]

<sup>16</sup>Then the twenty-four elders who sit before God on their thrones fell down on their faces and worshiped God,[^16] <sup>17</sup>saying:

&emsp;We give You thanks, O Lord God, the Almighty,<br>
&emsp;He Who Is, and Who Was,<br>
&emsp;for You have taken Your great power<br>
&emsp;and have begun to reign.[^17]<br>
&emsp;<sup>18</sup>And the nations were enraged,<br>
&emsp;and Your wrath has come,<br>
&emsp;and the time for the dead to be judged,<br>
&emsp;and for giving the reward to Your servants the prophets,<br>
&emsp;and to the saints, and to those who fear Your name,<br>
&emsp;the small and the great,<br>
&emsp;and for destroying those who destroy the earth.[^18]

<sup>19</sup>The sanctuary of God that is in heaven was opened, and the ark of His covenant was seen in His sanctuary; and there were flashes of lightning, and voices, and thunders, and an earthquake, and great hail.[^19]

---

[^1]: **11:1** “Reed”: Gr. κάλαμος, “reed, cane”, used as a measuring rod; Ezekiel’s was six long cubits, about 3 meters (10 feet) (Ezek 40:3–5; cf. 21:15–16). “Like a rod”: Gr. ὅμοιος ῥάβδῳ; ῥάβδος is “rod, staff”, the same word as in 2:27; 12:5; 19:15 (“rod of iron”). “I was told”: Gr. λέγων, lit. “saying”, with no expressed subject; the Textus Receptus reads “and the angel stood, saying”. “Sanctuary”: Gr. ναός (cf. 3:12; 7:15). “Measure”: cf. Ezek 40–42; Zech 2:1–2 (Heb. 2:5–6).

[^2]: **11:2** “Leave it out”: Gr. ἔκβαλε ἔξωθεν, lit. “cast it outside”. “Trample”: cf. Luke 21:24; Dan 8:13. “Forty-two months”: cf. 11:3; 12:6, 14; 13:5; Dan 7:25; 12:7.

[^3]: **11:3** “I will grant… to prophesy”: lit. “I will give… and they will prophesy” (cf. 3:21). “Witnesses”: Gr. μάρτυσιν (cf. 1:5; 2:13). “One thousand two hundred and sixty days”: cf. 12:6. “Sackcloth”: Gr. σάκκους (cf. 6:12).

[^4]: **11:4** “Olive trees… lampstands”: cf. Zech 4:2–3, 11–14; Rev 1:20. “Lord of the earth”: the Textus Receptus reads “God of the earth”.

[^5]: **11:5** “Fire… devours their enemies”: cf. 2 Kgs 1:10–12; Jer 5:14.

[^6]: **11:6** “Shut the sky… rain”: cf. 1 Kgs 17:1; Jas 5:17. “Waters… into blood”: cf. Ex 7:17–20. “Plague”: Gr. πληγή (cf. 9:18).

[^7]: **11:7** “Completed”: Gr. τελέσωσιν (cf. 10:7). “Beast”: Gr. θηρίον (cf. 13:1; 17:8; Dan 7:3, 7, 21). “Comes up out of the abyss”: cf. 9:1; 17:8.

[^8]: **11:8** “Dead body”: Gr. τὸ πτῶμα, singular, also in 11:9a; plural in 11:9b. Variants: the Textus Receptus reads “dead bodies” (also in 11:9a) and “our Lord”. “Will lie”: the Greek has no verb. “Spiritually”: Gr. πνευματικῶς. “Sodom and Egypt”: cf. Isa 1:9–10; Ezek 16:46–49.

[^9]: **11:9** “Some from”: Gr. ἐκ τῶν, lit. “from among” (cf. 5:9). “Peoples, and tribes, and tongues, and nations”: cf. 5:9; 7:9; 10:11. “Tomb”: Gr. μνῆμα.

[^10]: **11:10** “Tormented”: Gr. βασανίζω (cf. 9:5). Variant: the Byzantine text and the Textus Receptus read “will not permit” (11:9) and “will be glad”; the Textus Receptus also reads “will look” (11:9).

[^11]: **11:11** “Breath of life”: Gr. πνεῦμα ζωῆς, “spirit, breath of life” (cf. Gen 2:7, Septuagint πνοή; Ezek 37:5, 10); the Septuagint uses πνεῦμα ζωῆς in Gen 7:15 and Ezek 37:5. “Stood on their feet”: cf. Ezek 37:10.

[^12]: **11:12** “Come up here”: cf. 4:1. “In the cloud”: cf. 2 Kgs 2:11; Acts 1:9. Variant: the Byzantine text reads “And I heard”.

[^13]: **11:13** “Seven thousand names of men”: Gr. ὀνόματα ἀνθρώπων, that is, persons (cf. 3:4). Variant: the Byzantine text reads “in that day”. “The rest”: Gr. λοιποί (cf. 9:20). “Gave glory”: cf. 14:7; 16:9.

[^14]: **11:14** “Soon”: Gr. ταχύ (cf. 2:16; 22:7). “Woe”: cf. 8:13; 9:12.

[^15]: **11:15** “Has become”: Gr. ἐγένετο. “His Christ”: Gr. τοῦ χριστοῦ αὐτοῦ, “His Anointed” (cf. Ps 2:2; Dan 2:44; 7:14, 27). Variant: the Textus Receptus reads “the kingdoms of the world have become”.

[^16]: **11:16** “Before God”: the Byzantine text reads “before the throne of God”.

[^17]: **11:17** “He Who Is, and Who Was”: without “and Who Is Coming” (cf. 1:4, 8; 4:8); the Textus Receptus adds “and Who Is Coming”. “Have taken”: Gr. εἴληφας, perfect. “Have begun to reign”: Gr. ἐβασίλευσας, lit. “You reigned”.

[^18]: **11:18** “The nations were enraged”: cf. Ps 2:1–5; 99:1 [LXX 98:1]. “The time for the dead to be judged”: Gr. ὁ καιρὸς τῶν νεκρῶν κριθῆναι; καιρός = “time” (cf. 1:3; Dan 7:10, 22). “Reward”: Gr. μισθός (cf. 22:12). “The small and the great”: cf. 13:16; 19:5; 20:12; Ps 115:13 [LXX 113:21]. “Destroying those who destroy”: Gr. διαφθεῖραι… διαφθείροντας, also “corrupt”.

[^19]: **11:19** “Sanctuary… in heaven”: Gr. ναός (cf. 15:5; Heb 8:1–5; 9:24). “Ark of His covenant”: Gr. κιβωτὸς τῆς διαθήκης (cf. Ex 25:10–22; Deut 10:1–5; Heb 9:4). “Flashes of lightning, and voices, and thunders”: cf. 4:5; 8:5; 16:18. Variant: the Byzantine text reads “the ark of the covenant of the Lord” and omits “and an earthquake”.
